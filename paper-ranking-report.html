Notes on the Collection

Heavy duplication

20 works appear as 42 files — 22 redundant copies. Byte-identical pairs (Graphical Model for Bask/Basketball), arXiv-vs-conference versions (Meta-Analytics), and three copies of the Three Dimensions of Rebounding and Felsen & Lucey Body Shots papers each. Scores are held identical across copies.

Basketball dominates

Of 165 unique works: 85 basketball, 26 NFL, 12 soccer, 9 baseball, 8 hockey, 7 college football, plus betting, cricket and multi-sport. The NBA optical-tracking era (2012–2019) is the single densest cluster — and the least reproducible.

Best of the randoms/ folder

The 79-paper grab-bag skews lower (surveys, theses, a few non-research PDFs), but the standouts hold up:

Method & caveats

Scores come from reading the first ~2 pages of each PDF (title, abstract, intro, data section) — enough to judge contribution, data source and method, not to audit results. Eight scanned/slide-only PDFs were scored from the filename and known literature. Treat these as a fast, directional triage, not peer review.

Provenance — read before implementing

Eight of these are held as text only, with no PDF. SAGE’s version of record is Cloudflare-blocked from datacenter IPs and most of these have no preprint, so the text came from the published HTML through a reader proxy: prose and tables are as published, but equations are lost and figures and appendices are absent. Check any formula against the publisher page. One further paper (Zuccolotto et al. 2017) carries no Creative Commons licence and is held as a metadata stub only; its score was assigned from the abstract alone and is lower-confidence, in the same class as the eight scanned PDFs noted in the method above.

What the batch shows

Two pairs here ask near-identical questions with different rigour, and the ranking gap is entirely inferential discipline rather than data or technique. Glazer (23) versus the naive league-average translation she benchmarks against: same data, but a control group turns a directionally wrong adjustment into a causal estimate. mWAR (20) versus the NBA head-coach paper (14): both read a residual against a roster baseline, but one tests that residual against a simulated null before naming it. In this literature the control group and the null test are worth more than the model.

The proprietary trap, reproduced inside one bibliography

Ten of these are Pelechrinis-authored — one researcher’s output, so not a sample of the field. Even so the internal split matches the collection-wide pattern exactly: within that subset the papers on public data score 6–8 on reproducibility while the two on contracted optical tracking score 3 and 4, with no relationship to idea quality. DeepHoops carries that subset’s highest novelty (8) and its second-lowest composite.
