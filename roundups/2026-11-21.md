One paper had slipped through earlier sweeps:

- [Volley3D: Unified 3D Full-Sequence Reconstruction from Monocular Broadcasts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38358) (Boussouf, Maglo, Chaouch & Saito; cs.CV, 28 Oct; **16/30**, N7/P4/R5).
  - **Method:** a physics-based pipeline that needs no 3D training labels. Pose-based jump spotting plus Newtonian interpolation places airborne players in 3D. A TCN splits 2D ball tracks into sub-trajectories between contacts, and a collision matcher picks the touching player and hand. Drag-aware ballistic equations, anchored to the reconstructed 3D hands, then lift the ball into 3D.
  - **Results:** court-depth drift for jumping players falls from 1.05 m to 0.23 m MAE (homography baseline, 50 jumps). Segmentation F1 is 0.898 at a 1-frame tolerance on TrackNetV3 detections, against 0.815 for HitNet/MonoTrack. Ball 3D RMSE is 0.27 m, against 1.56 m for MonoTrack, which fits the image plane but not depth.
  - **Caveat:** the 3D validation uses 10 rallies from VolliQ's static four-camera rig, so the broadcast-specific parts have no 3D ground truth. Annotations are promised; no code.
  - **Why it matters here:** it is one of few tracking pipelines for a sport with no public tracking data. The physics-anchoring idea (hand contact points as trajectory bounds) carries over to other airborne-ball sports.
