## 1. Sports Analytics Literature & Research

**New this run: 3 papers.** Wednesday's `/new` listing (18 Nov) had no sports papers. All three come from a check of revisions announced in the past week that earlier runs had not saved. Two of them are from the same Bristol group (Clegg & Cartlidge) and work on the same question: where does a forecast's edge over the market, or over Elo, come from?

- [Capturing Intransitive Dominance in Tennis Forecasting: A Graph Neural Network Approach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20454) (Clegg & Cartlidge; **19/30**, N7/P6/R6). Accepted in the *International Journal of Forecasting* (2027); v3, the revision after peer review, was announced on 11-11.
  - **Method:** players are nodes and past results are directed edges in a time-ordered graph, fed to a MagNet GNN, which can represent A>B>C>A cycles that a single rating number cannot. Data: 20,181 ATP and 19,985 WTA matches at Grand Slam, Finals, 1000 and 500 level, 2012 to Oct 2025, from tennis-data.co.uk, with Pinnacle odds as the market reference.
  - **Results:** alone it ties Weighted Elo (65.7% accuracy, Brier 0.214). A forecast-encompassing test shows it carries information Elo lacks, and an Elo+GNN combination beats Elo with a significant difference, most on intransitive matchups. It works even for players with no common opponents.
  - **Code:** none released, but the data is public.
- [A market-initialised accelerated failure time model for in-play football forecasting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16066) (Clegg, Song & Cartlidge; **18/30**, N6/P7/R5). v2 is a substantial revision (twice the evaluation sample, a revised model, new benchmarks), announced on 11-11.
  - **Method:** a Weibull AFT goal-arrival model. Team strengths are fit to Betfair kick-off 1X2 and over/under prices by least squares, and cumulative shots from FBref enter as a time-varying covariate.
  - **Results:** scored minute by minute over 280 EPL matches, it still trails Betfair's in-play prices (68.5% vs 69.5% accuracy, RPS 0.1325 vs 0.1281). A Poisson, a Cox process and a Bayesian birth process, each started from the same prices, score about the same, so the accuracy comes from starting at the market price. The choice of goal model matters little. A betting simulation returns +1.0% ROI over 37k bets, with a 95% CI of −3.8% to +5.9%.
  - **Reproducibility:** Betfair's historical in-play data is not freely available, so R=5.
- [Inference in a generalized Bradley-Terry model for paired comparisons with covariates and a growing number of subjects](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22472) (Yan; **17/30**, N5/P5/R7). v2 reworks the theory and was announced on 11-13.
  - **Theory:** a Bradley–Terry model with per-game covariates such as home/away. As the number of teams grows, the MLE is consistent and asymptotically normal. The covariate (home-field) estimate has an asymptotic bias but the team merits do not, so a plain Wald interval for home advantage is off-centre.
  - **Example:** the 2018–19 NBA home effect is 0.45 (SE 0.065).
