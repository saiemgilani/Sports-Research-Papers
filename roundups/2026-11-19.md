## 1. Sports Analytics Literature & Research

**New this run: 2 papers**, both from Thursday's `/new` listing (19 Nov). Neither is a game-outcome paper. One is about measurement for tracking data, the other about anti-doping statistics.

- [Training-free motion statistics match self-supervised behavior encoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24792) (Chen; **18/30**, N6/P5/R7).
  - **Method:** generic per-frame movement measurements (speeds, distances, heading changes), summarised over short fixed windows and reduced with PCA. These are compared with retrained and released self-supervised encoders (BAMS, hBehaveMAE) under a preregistered protocol: frozen representations of equal size and one shared classifier.
  - **Results:** the hand-built features win on CalMS21 mice (85.8 vs 82.4 macro F1), MABe22 mice (+3.4, 95% CI +2.1 to +4.7) and MABe22 flies (51.6 vs 48.2). They also win on **Shot7M2, a simulated basketball player** (68.8 vs 64.7, against the benchmark authors' own model). hBehaveMAE stays ahead on the shortest movements. Raw body-frame keypoints over the same windows already match the encoders on most of these benchmarks.
  - **Why it matters here:** learned tracking embeddings (baller2vec-style models, player-movement encoders) are often reported without a simple windowed-feature baseline. This paper shows the baseline can win. Code is promised with the conference version.
- [Bayesian Computation of Copula-based Multivariate Models with an Application to Doping Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24801) (Masillo, Viltanioti, Deliu & Liseo; **14/30**, N6/P5/R3).
  - **Method:** the Athlete Biological Passport's ADAPTIVE model treats haemoglobin and the OFF-score separately. A copula couples the two, and the paper fits that model two ways: a fully joint MCMC and a two-step Bayesian IFM.
  - **Results:** the joint MCMC is about 9× faster (17 s vs 159 s at n=500) and calibrates the dependence better. The two-step IFM keeps the marginal inference safer when the copula family is wrong. On NADO Italia profiles (23 elite athletes, 223 samples), both methods flag 85–86% of the samples from court-ruling doping cases (TAS/CAS, UCI) as atypical.
  - **Reproducibility:** the data is confidential and no code is released, so R=3.
