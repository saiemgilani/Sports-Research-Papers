## 1. Sports Analytics Literature & Research

**New this run: 4 papers.** Three of them were first submitted earlier in the year. Their revisions were announced on 11-16 or 11-17, and the library had no copy of any of them.

- [Who is who: injury rates between players and within each player in professional team sports](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11228) (Ricou & Mahony; **21/30**, N7/P7/R7). Observational study on public match records: 22,573 players in six sports with injury records, nine sports in all. In football an injury is a substitution coded as an injury. Between two players picked at random, one's usual injury rate is typically 1.83× the other's. The paper's main point is a warning about method. Simulations with no load effect still make heavy weeks look 1.05–2.19× riskier within each player's season, because an injury lowers that player's later load. Within-player "acute load" comparisons therefore need a null simulation before anyone reads them causally. Per-hour rates that compare players mix recent load with who carries it. Aggregated results and scripts are on Zenodo (10.5281/zenodo.24243290); no player-level records are shared.
- [Anomaly detection using surprisals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09318) (Hyndman & Frazier; **20/30**, N6/P5/R9). An anomaly is an observation with unusually low probability under a model, possibly the wrong one. Its score is the upper-tail probability of its surprisal, estimated empirically (with a DKW finite-sample guarantee) or from a GPD fit. The method catches "inlier" anomalies that sit in low-density gaps between modes, and it is applied to Test-cricket data among others. Code: R package `weird`, plus a reproducible `targets` repo.
- [Subarchetypal Analysis: A Semi-Supervised Framework for Archetypal Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19930) (Bolós, Mollar-Gumbau, Alcacer & Epifanio; **19/30**, N6/P5/R8). Archetypal and archetypoid analysis restricted to chosen subsets of the data. One worked case builds archetypes from the DEA-efficient frontier, which gives an "ideal efficient" NBA player per position from 2025–26 totals. R package `subarchetypes`; DEA through `deaR`.
- [New rules, old habits: across seven team sports, each league keeps its own way of using the rules](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05584) (Ricou; **18/30**, N6/P5/R7). Covers 101 leagues. After soccer's limit went from 3 to 5 substitutes, the 14 top leagues kept their substitution-usage order (ρ = 0.89, about as stable as one ordinary season to the next). A prediction for 12 second tiers, written before their records were read, held at ρ = 0.73; they ranked like their country's top league. In other sports, leagues kept their order in 4 of 8 tests. Rule-makers should judge a change league by league.
